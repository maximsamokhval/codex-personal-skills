# Thinking Tools Orchestrator

Master-framework for applying cognitive mental models and thinking tools (Untools, Dialog Guide) to solve complex engineering, product, and strategic problems deterministically.

## When to Use

- The user faces a recurring problem, system deadlock, or architectural dilemma.
- The user asks to analyze a strategy, business decision, or technical challenge using mental models.
- When you need to systematically strip away assumptions, find hidden risks, and generate robust alternatives.

## Core Protocol: The 4-Step Decision & Deconstruction Engine

When invoked, guide the analysis through four sequential phases. Do not jump to conclusions or recommend tools without stepping through the problem structure.

### Phase 1: Symptom & Root Cause Diagnosis (Iceberg Model)
Separate surface events from foundational drivers:
1. **Events**: What is the immediate visible symptom or pain point?
2. **Patterns**: How often does this repeat and what cycle does it follow?
3. **Structures**: What systems, incentives, or lack of feedback loops maintain this pattern?
4. **Mental Models**: What underlying assumptions or false beliefs are driving the behavior?

### Phase 2: Reality Stripping (First-Principles Thinking)
Erase dogma, market consensus, and "how things are usually done":
1. Break the problem down to undisputed foundational facts and physical/logical constraints.
2. Rebuild the solution strictly upward from these primitives.

### Phase 3: Risk & Consequence Stress-Testing (Inversion & Second-Order Thinking)
Expose blind spots across time and failure modes:
1. **Inversion**: "How could we deliberately guarantee a total failure or make this problem infinitely worse?" (Reverse engineering the risks and Single Points of Failure).
2. **Second-Order Thinking**: Ask "And then what?" for the proposed solution at 6 to 12 months out. What unexpected ripple effects or bottlenecks will emerge?

### Phase 4: Alternative Generation (Rule of Three)
Prevent confirmation bias and premature convergence:
- Never settle on the first "obvious" idea. Force generation of at least **three distinct, viable architectural or strategic paths** with explicit trade-offs.

## Output Format

Structure your analysis clearly using concise markdown, highlighting actionable insights and trade-offs for each phase.
